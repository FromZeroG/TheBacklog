    Ruinarch

    A villain-sim god game with a clever indirect-manipulation hook that runs out of tricks.

    Platform PC Genre God Game / Colony Sim Released Apr 2023 (1.0) Logged 18 Hrs
    Official art · via Steam
    63% Mixed Bag

    Ruinarch casts you as an overlord who never touches the villagers directly. Curses, planted rumors, captured monsters, and environmental spells nudge a town toward ruin while its people react to manipulation you never show your hand on. Three overlord archetypes, Lich, Puppetmaster, and Ravager, each build toward that goal differently, and villagers carry hidden traits like alcoholic or kleptomaniac that the game's systems can exploit in specific, memorable ways.

    The indirect-influence design is the real hook: villagers form relationships and grudges on their own, and prodding those relationships, rather than issuing orders, produces stories reviewers consistently call the game's best feature. Capturing enemies and repurposing them, turning a slain Angel into a Fallen Angel for your own side, gives thematic army-building a payoff beyond stat numbers.

    The toolkit doesn't grow with the playtime. Archetypes draw from overlapping spell pools, so a player who has mastered one path finds the others familiar rather than fresh, and Roundtable Co-Op flagged how quickly the endgame turns into waiting on mana regen instead of making new decisions. Corrupted villagers don't hide their crimes convincingly, which undercuts the secret-puppetmaster fantasy the marketing leans on. UI gaps compound it: no minimap, a restrictive minimum camera zoom, and tutorialization that reviewers called insufficient for a systems-heavy game.

    Steam rates it Mixed at 62 percent across roughly 1,300 reviews, and the current playerbase has shrunk to single digits concurrent, down from a 2020 Early Access peak over a thousand. Critic coverage is close to nonexistent: one 2020 outlet review during Early Access is the only score on record, so there's no real aggregate to lean on. The 1.0 patch in 2023 added monsters, ideologies, and villager actions on top of that early build, but the core complaints about depth held up.

    Worth a sale price. The manipulation-not-control hook is worth experiencing at least once, and the small, dedicated community left playing it backs that up, but don't expect the toolkit to keep expanding the longer you stay.
