    Timberborn

    Mechanistry's lumberpunk beaver city-builder spent nearly four years in Early Access before reaching 1.0, and the drought-and-verticality hook still carries the whole thing.

    Platform PC Genre City Builder / Colony Sim Released Mar 2026 Expected Playtime ~23 Hrs+
    Official art · via Steam
    97% Delivered

    Mechanistry's Timberborn puts players in charge of a beaver civilization rebuilding after humanity's disappearance, balancing vertical dam-and-canal construction against a recurring drought cycle that threatens to dry out every water source on the map. It spent nearly four years in Early Access before reaching 1.0 in March 2026, and the response since launch has matched the reputation it built up over that run.

    What Works

    The drought system is still the game's signature idea, and it holds up at full release. Water reserves built up during the wet season become the only thing standing between a colony and collapse once a dry spell hits, and the vertical building tools, dams, canals, elevated platforms, turn terrain that would sink a flatter city-builder into a real strategic resource. Softpedia's review praised the "interesting concepts, mainly the vertical building and drought mechanics" and found the balance between construction and survival pressure well-tuned throughout.

    The numbers back that up. Steam holds Timberborn at 96 percent Overwhelmingly Positive across more than 23,000 reviews, with the most recent month sitting at the same 96 percent, and Metacritic's critic aggregate lands at 85 from 12 reviews with zero negative scores recorded. The 1.0 update also added a full automation system, twenty logic-based buildings that let players script routines like auto-closing floodgates during a contamination event, which PC Gamer flagged as arguably the biggest addition of the whole release.

    What Doesn't

    Softpedia's review, one of the more detailed critical takes available, lists real complaints under the praise: the world's lore stays thin, the landscape reads as visually drab and blocky, research-point progression drags into a grind, and there's no higher speed setting for players who've already mastered the early game. The beavers themselves, despite carrying individual stats, come across as interchangeable rather than characters worth knowing.

    Worth flagging separately: Metacritic's user score sits at a mixed 6.5, but that number comes from only 26 ratings, a sliver next to Steam's 23,000-plus review base. It reads as a small-sample quirk rather than a real signal splitting critics from players, and Steam's much larger and more recent sample is the one worth trusting here.

    Timberborn earns its four years of Early Access. The drought-and-verticality hook still carries the whole game at 1.0, and the new automation layer gives long-term colonies real systems to design instead of chores to repeat. Thin lore and slow research pacing keep it out of the site's rarest tier, but at 96 percent sustained through launch, this is one of the more confidently finished city-builders reviewed here.
