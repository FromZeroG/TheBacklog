    Palworld

    Two years past the meme, Palworld turned into an actual game worth two hundred hours, cracked base and all.

    Platform PC Genre Survival Crafting Released Jan 2024 (1.0: Jul 2026) Logged 200+ Hrs
    Official art · via Steam
    104% Delivered

    Palworld launched in January 2024 as a joke half the internet was in on: Pokemon, but you can arm them with rifles and put them on an assembly line. Two and a half years and a full 1.0 release later, I've put in two hundred hours, and the joke stopped being the reason I kept playing somewhere around hour thirty.

    The core trick still works because Pocketpair built real systems underneath the meme. Drop a Pal at a crafting station and it works the job it's suited for, no micromanagement required, and the base you build around that idea turns into an actual production line instead of a decoration project. Breeding and customization go deeper than they need to, especially after the Feybreak expansion added new zones worth building a fresh base around. None of it feels like it's coasting on the initial hype anymore, and the 1.0 update backed that up with seventy-two new Pals and new areas, added for free to a game people already bought.

    Play it with friends and it gets better. Co-op is where the game clicks hardest, splitting base-building and boss runs across a group instead of grinding solo. Steam agrees: sentiment sits around 96 percent positive, and it's held there through the transition out of Early Access instead of sliding the way most survival-crafting hype games do once the algorithm moves on.

    It's not without real problems. Base building itself is thinner than the genre's best, described accurately by PC Gamer as a fairly boring set of Legos once you've built your third one. The 1.0 launch brought stuttering and frame drops that shouldn't happen to a game two years into development, and multiplayer still desyncs players' positions on dedicated servers often enough to be a known issue rather than a rare one. There's also Nintendo's ongoing patent lawsuit hanging over the game, though the claims have narrowed considerably and cover older mechanics Pocketpair already patched out.

    It's great. Two hundred hours in and I'm still finding new things to build, which is more than I can say for almost anything else in this genre.
