    SNKRX

    A three-dollar auto-battler wearing Snake's skin, with more build depth than its price tag lets on.

    Platform PC Genre Auto-Battler Roguelite Released May 2021 Logged 14 Hrs
    Official art · via Steam
    97% Delivered

    SNKRX steers a snake made of hero units instead of blank segments, auto-attacking enemies as it grows. Every hero belongs to one of a dozen-plus classes, and stacking heroes of the same class unlocks synergy bonuses that turn a run into real build-crafting rather than a length contest. Solo developer a327ex built the whole thing, open-sourced it on GitHub, and still sells it for three dollars.

    Runs last twenty rounds with passives offered every fourth one, and New Game+ modes raise the snake's size cap for players chasing more. The steering itself holds up as an arcade skill: tight keyboard-and-mouse control and a wall-bounce mechanic that reviewers call satisfying on their own, before the build-crafting even enters the picture. Higher Plain Games put it in the same sentence as Vampire Survivors as a budget-title benchmark, and the comparison holds: SNKRX helped shape the genre wave that followed it.

    The developer is candid about the game's ceiling. In his own post-launch writeup, he says most players engage hard for a week or two and then move on, admitting there's "not enough content" to hold anyone for hundreds of hours. Difficulty swings by build in ways that read as inconsistent to some players rather than deliberate, a glass-cannon build can end a boss fight before it attacks once, a defensive build tanks the same fight for minutes, and class balance has whiplashed over patches: healers and Psykers went from dismissed as weak to proven strong by a streamer, then got rebalanced in a way the community pushed back on. Shop RNG can sour a run before it starts if the gold or items on offer don't line up with your build.

    Steam rates it Very Positive at roughly 94 percent across more than 4,000 reviews, a number that's held steady for years. Critic coverage barely exists; Metacritic shows no critic reviews at all, and its listed "user score" comes from only six raters, too small to mean anything next to Steam's own thousands. This is a Steam-community reputation, not a press one, earned the hard way through years of the developer studying other studios' post-launch update cadences and shipping accordingly.

    Buy it. At three dollars, the build variety alone clears the bar, and the developer's own honesty about its content ceiling is more reason to trust the rest of what he built.
