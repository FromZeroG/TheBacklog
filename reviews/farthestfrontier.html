    Farthest Frontier

    Three years in Early Access built a city builder with a fully physical economy, undercut by weak combat and a slow opening.

    Platform PC Genre City Builder / Survival Released Aug 2022 (1.0: Oct 2025) Logged 85 Hrs
    Official art · via Steam
    88% Delivered

    Farthest Frontier spent three years in Early Access before Crate Entertainment called it finished in October 2025, and the extra time shows in the one system every other city builder fakes: the economy is fully physical. Carts haul goods down real roads, and laborers walk to the job instead of teleporting there. Nothing moves until someone moves it.

    That physicality runs all the way down to the dirt. Each field tracks its own soil nutrients, crop rotation, and weed buildup, so farming stops being a slider you set once and forget. Disease isn't random either: skip stockpiling firewood and your smokehouses stop preserving meat, and sickness follows the shortage instead of a dice roll. Population grows and ages on its own instead of getting recruited on demand, so headcount and workforce become two numbers you have to plan around separately. Raiders target your richest stockpiles, and your soldiers have to physically reach the armory before they can fight back, turning defense into a logistics problem instead of a button press.

    The early hours drag. Advancing from one wall tier to the next means grinding through a resource queue with little else to do but wait, and once a town stabilizes around five hundred people, the tension that made the opening hours tense mostly evaporates. Combat is the weak link across the board: raiders drip-feed into your defenses against AI that Steam reviewers have called out directly as thin, and the interface for placing buildings still leans on clunky manual sliders instead of anything more direct.

    Eighty-six percent of nearly eleven thousand reviewers still call it Very Positive, even with the last thirty days cooling to seventy-nine as the 1.0 launch hype settles. This is one of the best-simulated city builders available right now, weak combat and slow opening hours included.

    Buy it. The simulation depth carries the slow start and the thin combat. Give it the first ten hours it needs to get going before you decide.
