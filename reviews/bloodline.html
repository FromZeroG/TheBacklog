    The Bloodline

    An ambitious solo-dev open world that oversells the "open" part and still owes you a stable frame rate.

    Platform PC Genre Action RPG / Open-World Released Oct 2023 (Early Access) Logged 20 Hrs
    Official art · via Steam
    65% Mixed Bag

    Shieldbearer Studios built The Bloodline mostly alone, which explains both why it's impressive and why it's still held together with duct tape two years into Early Access. You wake up a nobody in a medieval fantasy world with a sword, a horse, and a store page that calls the world "open." Play for ten hours and you'll notice the map is actually a string of zones stitched together by invisible walls, not the seamless sandbox the marketing implies.

    What works, works well. The skill system rewards nearly everything you do: swing an axe enough and you get better at swinging axes, sneak past enough guards and stealth levels up on its own. Over five hundred skills sit behind that loop, and I never ran out of new ones to chase. Movement holds up too. Grappling hooks, wall-runs, and zip-lines turn getting around into its own small game, and village-building lets you stack a base without a carry-weight limit nagging you the whole time.

    Then the seams show. I lost an entire afternoon's session to a bug that wiped my inventory and never gave it back. The HUD vanished twice for no reason I could find, and reloading a zone resets every enemy I'd already cleared, so "exploring" a lot of the map just means fighting the same three bandits on a loop. A Steam thread has multiple players reporting frame drops into single digits on a 3080 Ti, which shouldn't happen to a game with these visuals.

    None of it ruins the game. It just keeps The Bloodline from being more than what it currently is, a genuinely ambitious project from a small team that hasn't finished ironing out the basics. You can throw a weapon at an enemy and watch it disappear instead of picking it back up, which tells you exactly where the polish budget went.

    It's fine. Worth the sale price for the skill system and the traversal alone, but go in expecting Early Access bugs, because that's what you're buying.
