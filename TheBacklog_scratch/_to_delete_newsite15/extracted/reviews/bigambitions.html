    Big Ambitions

    Hovgaard Games' business sandbox spent three and a half years in Early Access before its 1.0 launch, and the launch mostly delivers on the wait.

    Platform PC Genre Business Sim / Life Sim Released Aug 2026 (1.0) Expected Playtime ~22 Hrs+
    Official art · via Steam
    89% Delivered

    Big Ambitions puts you in New York with nothing but startup capital and a choice: open a corner shop, start a factory, buy up real estate, or all three at once. Hovgaard Games spent three and a half years building it out in Early Access before the full 1.0 release at the end of August 2026 added a seventh neighborhood, The Hamptons, along with new furniture and activities. The sandbox premise stayed the same across that stretch. The execution underneath it is what improved.

    What Works

    The scope is the pitch, and it holds up. You can run a single retail store, chain together a supply network from raw goods to shelf, or build out apartment complexes and manage tenants, often all in the same save. Movies Games and Tech called it "a business sim on a level I've not seen before," and the range of playstyles it supports backs that up: nothing forces you into one strategy, and the systems for buying property, hiring staff, and managing production lines all connect to each other rather than sitting in separate menus.

    Steam reviewers agree at a rare scale for a business sim: 91 percent Very Positive across roughly 5,650 all-time reviews, and 87 percent Very Positive in the 30 days after 1.0 launched, with concurrent players jumping from around 1,000 to nearly 8,000 the week of release. That's a launch a lot of Early Access games never get.

    What Doesn't

    The economy doesn't push back. A single well-placed store can clear five figures a day within the first few in-game weeks, loan payments can be minimized down to bare interest with no real downside, and from there expansion becomes a matter of repeating the same setup rather than solving a new problem. A long-running Steam Community thread from a player past 150 hours put it plainly: profits run too high, too early, and the loan system carries no real consequence once you understand it.

    The other recurring complaint is time spent on busywork rather than decisions: unpacking a new store's inventory means clicking through dozens of individual items one at a time, a task players in that same thread describe as a hard stop on momentum rather than a meaningful part of running a business. Staff currently show up and work; there's no theft, no absenteeism, no conflict to manage, which leaves the promised "life sim" side of the game closer to a checklist than a simulation once the store is stocked.

    Big Ambitions earned its 1.0 with genuine breadth, even if the economy underneath that breadth still needs friction. There's enough here to build a real business empire the way you picture it, and the tedious setup tasks and the lack of any real financial risk are the two things standing between a good business sim and a great one.
