    Panorama

    A cozy hex-tile city builder with real charm, running out of content well before the difficulty catches up to it.

    Platform PC Genre Puzzle City Builder Released May 2023 Expected Playtime ~4 Hrs
    Official art · via Steam
    87% Delivered

    Panorama, styled Pan'orama on its own store page, hands you hexagonal terrain tiles and asks you to fit them together into a living landscape: match biomes for points, and the world responds with birds, wheat fields swaying, tractors working the land, boats crossing rivers. An undo button keeps the placement puzzle forgiving instead of punishing, and a separate diorama mode drops the scoring entirely for players who want to build without the pressure.

    Fifty-two collectible structures and quest tiles give the unlock progression a real shape, and the lo-poly pastel art paired with satisfying placement sound design is the reason reviewers describe losing four hours without noticing. It's a small, tightly-scoped game that does its central idea well.

    The campaign runs out fast: Niche Gamer clocked the main mode at around two hours, with Creative mode adding little beyond free-building on top. The tutorial is bare-bones and reviewers found actual typos in it, and one outlet reported it breaking outright during their playthrough. A rotating camera view makes precise tile placement more fiddly than it needs to be. Steam's own discussion boards document a real difficulty wall around 60 to 65 buildings, where the final blueprints become close to impossible to unlock and the tone shifts from relaxing to stressful, a complaint the developer acknowledged and promised to address. Multiple reviewers also compare it directly and unfavorably to Dorfromantik, the game its hex-tile formula draws from, saying Panorama doesn't establish its own identity on top of it.

    Steam rates it Very Positive at 89 percent across roughly 1,100 reviews, with a smaller, thinner recent sample holding at 76 percent. No real critic aggregate exists on PC; coverage stayed limited to a handful of indie outlets, Niche Gamer, LadiesGamers, Higher Plain Games, rather than a major press consensus.

    Buy it. A short, well-crafted puzzle for a relaxing afternoon, less convincing as a game you'll return to once the unlock list and the difficulty wall both show up.
