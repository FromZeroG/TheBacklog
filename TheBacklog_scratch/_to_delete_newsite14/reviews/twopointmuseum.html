    Two Point Museum

    The series' newest entry sends curators out into the field to dig up their own exhibits, and the whole formula is better for having somewhere new to go.

    Platform PC Genre Management Sim / City Builder Released Mar 2025 Expected Playtime ~47 Hrs
    Official art · via Steam
    95% Delivered

    Two Point Museum is the third management sim to come out of Two Point Studios, and the first to send its staff outside the building before the building even matters. Hospital and Campus both start with a plot of land and a to-do list of interior systems; Museum starts with an empty room and an expedition map, because before there's anything to display, someone has to go dig it up. Curators lead field teams into fossil digs, haunted sites, and fantasy realms to bring back the fish, artifacts, and creatures that fill the exhibit halls, and that one structural change reworks how the whole loop feels compared to its two predecessors.

    What Works

    The expedition system is a real structural addition, not a coat of paint over the same building sim. Sending a team out, watching them report back with specimens, damage, or a cryptic warning about what they found, then deciding whether to fund a bigger dig, gives the game an entire acquisition layer that Hospital and Campus never needed to think about. It threads a genuine exploration thread through a genre that's normally pure floor-plan optimization, and critics and players both singled it out as the freshest idea the series has had since Hospital first proved the format worked.

    Everything downstream of that hook is the strongest version of the Two Point toolkit to date. Metacritic's aggregate (84) and Steam's player base (95 percent all-time, climbing to 96 percent Overwhelmingly Positive in the most recent stretch) both land higher than either Hospital or Campus managed, and the reasons line up with what's on screen: sharper exhibit variety, a livelier visitor simulation, and a science-and-restoration research tree deep enough to justify a museum running for dozens of hours without repeating itself.

    What Doesn't

    The genre's long-standing weak spot, crowd and staff pathing, hasn't gone away. Visitors and curators alike can get stuck on routes that look clean on the overhead view and aren't once dozens of guests are cutting across the same gallery at once, and a jammed hallway during a busy exhibition opening is the moment where a management sim needs its AI to hold up, and it doesn't always.

    The late game asks for a real management workload, staffing multiple simultaneous expeditions, restoration queues, and exhibit rotations, and reviewers who praised the core loop still flagged the point where that workload tips from engaging into a genuine grind. It's less a design flaw than a ceiling: Museum gives players more systems to run at once than either previous game, and running all of them well by the third or fourth museum takes real sustained attention rather than the loose, ambient management the earlier games allowed.

    The best entry the format has produced. Two Point Museum takes a formula that was already well-liked twice over and finds a new hook for it, expeditions worth the pathing headaches and the late-game grind they bring along. Anyone burned out on Hospital or Campus should look here first before writing off the series.
