    BALL x PIT

    Kenny Sun's breakout-roguelite hybrid turns one bouncing ball into a screen-filling tower-defense spectacle within a few runs.

    Platform PC Genre Action Roguelite Released Oct 2025 Expected Playtime ~19 Hrs
    Official art · via Steam
    96% Delivered

    BALL x PIT throws a straightforward premise, bounce a ball through breakout-style bricks, into a roguelite structure built by Kenny Sun and published by Devolver Digital. Runs stack towers of automated ball-launchers between short bursts of manual aiming, and the game escalates from a single bouncing ball into screen-filling chaos within a few runs. It released in October 2025 to Devolver's usual mix of quiet confidence and a viral pre-launch trailer, and the response has matched the hype since.

    What Works

    The core loop earns the comparisons to Vampire Survivors that followed it around before release. Early runs feel almost too simple, one ball, one paddle, but new characters, fruit-based buildings, and passive upgrades stack fast enough that a run can spiral from careful aiming into dozens of ricocheting balls inside twenty minutes. PC Gamer's review called it a roller coaster ride of ball-bouncing action that loves to break its own rules, pointing to a stream of new mechanics the reviewer described as constantly unlocking things that feel borderline illegal.

    The response backs that up with real numbers. Metacritic's aggregate sits at 84 from 42 critic reviews, and Steam's audience rates it 95 percent Overwhelmingly Positive across more than 15,000 reviews, with the most recent 30 days holding at 91 percent. That kind of alignment between critics and players is rare for a roguelite this new, and it lines up with the well-documented viral moment around the reveal trailer that turned into real sales once the game shipped.

    What Doesn't

    The character roster doesn't land evenly. A handful of playable characters, the Cogitator among the most cited, contribute mechanics that reviewers and players call unfun outright, and picking one of them on a given run can flatten the escalation the rest of the cast delivers. The strategic layer underneath the spectacle stays thin: once a run gets going, success comes down mostly to which upgrades appear rather than a deep decision tree.

    Longevity is the other real limit. Most players see the bulk of what BALL x PIT offers inside roughly twenty hours, at which point New Game+ becomes the main source of new challenge, and reviewers who wanted a deeper meta-progression system to chase past that point were left wanting one. For a roguelite in this price range, twenty hours before the well starts to feel familiar sits on the short side of the genre.

    A roguelite that earns its viral moment. BALL x PIT takes a premise simple enough to explain in one sentence and builds a run structure that keeps surprising for the twenty-odd hours it takes to see everything, weak characters and thin strategic depth included. Anyone burned out on the genre's more cerebral entries should treat this as the chaotic palate cleanser it's built to be.
